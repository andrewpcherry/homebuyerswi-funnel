We Are Your Local Milwaukee Market Experts!


Milwaukee’s homes are notably old. About 34.67% of homes were built before 1939, and another 16.48% were built between 1970 and 1999. Only about 7.52% of the housing stock was built in 2000 or later. This makes Milwaukee one of the older housing markets in the Midwest, with the majority of homes dating from the early-to-mid 20th century.

Common Problems in Milwaukee Homes
Given the age of the housing stock, buyers and renters should be aware of several recurring issues:

Lead paint: Lead-based paint is present in at least 100,000 Milwaukee homes. Older homes, typically those built before 1978, may contain deteriorated lead-based paint or friable asbestos that require qualified remediation.
Lead pipes: Some residents are also concerned about lead pipes in the water supply, though the water is treated to reduce lead levels.
Asbestos: Homes built before the 1970s often contain asbestos in insulation, flooring, and roofing materials, which can cause serious lung disease if disturbed.
Outdated electrical and plumbing: Most houses built in the early 20th century were not wired for the number of electronics, lights, and appliances common today. Older homes may also have lead plumbing that requires filtration or full replumbing.
Water infiltration and foundation issues: Inspectors frequently flag cracked or missing siding, rotted trim, failed caulking, poor grading, and hardscape issues that direct water toward foundations — all common in aging Milwaukee homes.
Chimney and roof deterioration: Cracked flue liners, loose crown mortar, and missing chimney caps are frequently cited defects, along with window and door failures involving rot and broken seals.
