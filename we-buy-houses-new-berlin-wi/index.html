New Berlin, Wisconsin is a Waukesha County suburb of about 40,000–40,500 people, forming a neat six-by-six-mile square southeast of the county. It was founded in 1840, stayed largely agricultural (alfalfa and apples) until the 1940s-50s, then grew explosively as Milwaukee’s suburbs expanded: population went from 5,334 in 1950 to 15,788 by 1960, and the city incorporated in 1959 specifically to avoid being annexed by neighboring Brookfield. That mid-century boom, followed by continued growth through the 1990s-2000s (new-home permits peaked at 205 in 1999 and 120+ in 2000) and still-active luxury infill today (33 permits in 2025 at an average cost of about $827,000), means the housing stock is a genuine blend — a large mid-century-through-1980s core plus meaningful newer, higher-end construction. That puts the typical home age somewhere in the 40-60 year range, skewing a bit older than younger overall.

Common housing issues in New Berlin:

  • Aging systems in the mid-century core — homes from the 1960s-80s boom era are old enough that roofing, electrical, and plumbing are common repair items, as in most suburbs of that vintage.
  • A documented history of exclusionary zoning limiting affordable/multifamily housing — in 2011 the U.S. Department of Justice sued the city under the Fair Housing Act after its planning commission reversed approval of a 180-unit affordable housing project amid public opposition the DOJ alleged was rooted in racial stereotyping, and after the city tightened zoning to block future affordable projects. The case settled with a consent decree requiring the city to permit the project, revise its zoning, fund a $75,000 Housing Trust Fund, and adopt fair-housing training and compliance measures — a legacy that has shaped the city’s relatively limited stock of affordable and rental housing.
  • Cold-climate maintenance load — as with most southeastern Wisconsin housing stock, ice damming, aging furnaces, and basement moisture/waterproofing are recurring seasonal maintenance concerns, especially in older homes.
