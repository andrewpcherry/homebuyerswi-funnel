Greenfield, Wisconsin is a Milwaukee County suburb of about 37,000–38,000 people, bordering Milwaukee on the city’s southwest side. It has a slow-burn development history: settled in 1839 as the Town of Kinnickinnic, renamed Greenfield in 1841, and largely agricultural through the 1800s — but it didn’t incorporate as a city until 1957, the last Milwaukee-area municipality to do so, and only after losing roughly two-thirds of its original land to earlier incorporations by neighbors like West Allis, Greendale, and Hales Corners. Most of its housing stock is a product of the postwar suburban boom that followed: population surged from about 17,600 in 1960 to over 24,400 by 1970, and more than 25% of the city’s existing single-family homes were built in that single decade. That puts the typical Greenfield home at roughly 50-plus years old, with a scattering of much older pre-1940s subdivisions and some newer infill (as recent as the 2000s) mixed in.

Common housing issues in Greenfield:

  • Sewer inflow/infiltration and basement flooding — this is a well-documented, current issue: stormwater intrudes into aging sanitary sewer laterals (adding an estimated 40-50 gallons per minute per leaky lateral), causing sewage backups into homes during heavy rain. This came to a head in the historic August 2025 Milwaukee-area flooding, when Greenfield saw serious basement flooding and sewage backups, with some residents losing major appliances and belongings to floodwater.
  • Basement vulnerability generally — with many homes dating to the 1950s-70s, older basement waterproofing and drainage systems are a recurring weak point independent of any single storm event.
  • Aging systems from the postwar boom era — roofing, electrical, and plumbing typical of homes built in the 1950s-70s are common inspection and repair items.
  • Foundation and structural repair demand — the presence of multiple local foundation-repair contractors serving the area suggests this is an active, ongoing concern for the older housing stock.
  • Water-main and utility infrastructure age — Greenfield has also seen isolated water-main breaks flood basements, pointing to broader aging-infrastructure strain alongside the sewer issues.
