How To Avoid Probate Court In Wisconsin

How To Avoid Probate Court In Wisconsin

November 5, 2024

By nategifford

Nobody wants to have to go through the probate process, especially if they plan to sell the inherited property once their loved ones have passed. Dealing with the probate process can seem overwhelming. In our latest guide, discover how to avoid probate court in Wisconsin and learn simple steps you can take now to protect your family and your property.

Whether you plan to keep the inherited property or sell it after a relative passes away, it’s important to plan ahead. By being proactive, you can help your loved ones avoid lengthy court proceedings and expensive fees. In turn, your family can focus on healing and moving forward rather than getting bogged down in legalities.

Why Avoid Probate?

There are many reasons people aim to avoid probate in Wisconsin. For starters, probate can quickly become costly, with attorney charges, executor fees, and court costs piling up. While some executors may waive their fees to keep the peace within the family, other charges cannot be skipped. Beyond the expenses, probate also demands a lot of time. Assets must be officially accounted for, and proper notices must be sent to heirs. Before any inheritance is distributed to beneficiaries, all debts of the estate must be settled. This process can feel drawn out, adding stress to an already difficult period.

Another factor is privacy. Probate records can be public, meaning personal details about your loved one’s estate could become available for anyone to see. By planning to bypass probate, you can protect your family’s financial information and maintain a greater degree of confidentiality. Ultimately, avoid probate in Wisconsin if you want to spare your family lengthy paperwork, protect their privacy, and help them save on unwanted legal fees.

Using A Trust

One straightforward way to keep your assets out of probate is by setting up a trust. Specifically, a revocable living trust can give you the power to decide exactly which assets are included and how they will be handled once you are gone. Imagine a trust like a basket (or a bucket) into which you place different items, such as real estate, stocks, or personal valuables. By transferring ownership of these assets to the trust while you are still capable of making decisions, you ensure they are not subject to probate once you pass away.

It’s crucial to establish the trust at a time when you are of sound mind. This approach allows you to make thoughtful choices about who should benefit from your estate. Plus, because assets in a living trust skip probate, your heirs can inherit faster and with fewer complications. Working with a financial expert can help you structure your trust to fit your unique needs, especially if your estate holds a variety of property types.

Joint Ownership

Joint ownership is another helpful method for families trying to avoid probate court in Wisconsin. When two or more people buy property together, they can set it up as joint tenancy with the right of survivorship. This arrangement means that if one owner passes away, the other automatically becomes the sole owner of the property. No probate is required for that transfer.

While this strategy is useful, it must be arranged when you purchase the property. Adding someone to the deed after the fact can require added paperwork and fees. If you know you want the property to pass smoothly to another owner, be sure to plan the arrangement at the time of purchase. A little forethought can spare your heirs from dealing with the court system and help them hold onto the property without major legal hurdles.

Beneficiary Designations

Certain financial accounts and insurance policies allow you to name a beneficiary. For instance, bank accounts and life insurance often include a space to list who should receive the funds if you pass away. By filling out this information—and keeping it up to date—you can make sure these assets are transferred directly to the designated beneficiary. This avoids the court altogether.

In some areas, you may also create what’s known as a transfer-on-death (TOD) deed or certificate for real estate. This legal document ensures the property will instantly pass to a named individual when you die. To prevent surprises or misunderstandings, regularly review your beneficiary choices—especially after significant life changes such as divorce, remarriage, or the passing of a loved one. Neglecting updates can lead to unintended heirs or force property into the probate process, which defeats the goal of a swift, worry-free transfer.

Giving Inheritance Away

Another way to reduce what goes through the probate process is to give away part of your estate before you pass. Items that no longer belong to you do not need to be probated. The federal gift tax laws allow people to give a certain amount each year without tax penalties—often around $11,000 or so per person annually, though this figure can change. Spreading out gifts over a few years may significantly reduce the size of your estate that remains at your death.

This strategy requires planning and communication. You want to ensure the gifts you give are both meaningful and practical. Giving away large items in advance means loved ones can enjoy them sooner, and your estate shrinks in a legal sense, making the probate process either simpler or unnecessary.

Avoiding Probate for Smaller Estates

Not every estate is large or complicated. In some states, if the estate is valued under a certain threshold, the probate process can be sped up or bypassed through simpler legal forms. This threshold varies by location, so check local laws in Wisconsin to see if a small-estate procedure might apply.

These simplified processes often require filling out a special affidavit or a short form to claim assets. While this shortcut can save time and money, it’s only available if your estate’s total value is under the designated limit. If you’re uncertain about the rules in your area, consider speaking with a local professional who knows exactly what is required to finalize an estate without unnecessary court steps.

Planning Now for Peace of Mind

Ultimately, making arrangements to avoid probate in Wisconsin can save your family from hassles and stress after you’re gone. From setting up a trust to adding beneficiaries to your accounts, these small actions can have a big impact. Whether you own a single-family home or multiple investment properties, there are ways to organize your assets so your wishes are honored with the least amount of red tape.
