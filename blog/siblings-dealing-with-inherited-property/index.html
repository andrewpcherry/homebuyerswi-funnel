5 Tips for Siblings Dealing With Inherited Property in Wisconsin

5 Tips for Siblings Dealing With Inherited Property in Wisconsin

November 5, 2024

By nategifford

Have you inherited property in Wisconsin? While inheriting a family home or other property can be a significant financial benefit, it often comes with unexpected challenges. For many, the emotional toll of losing a loved one is compounded by the complexities of managing an inherited property, especially when multiple siblings are involved. Without clear guidance or preparation, dealing with such property can become a source of stress, confusion, and even conflict. From legal paperwork to financial obligations, the process can feel overwhelming during an already difficult time.

Fond memories of family gatherings at the inherited property may clash with the reality of shared ownership responsibilities. Whether it’s maintenance, taxes, or deciding the property’s future, siblings must navigate a range of decisions together. Even families that work harmoniously can find the estate settlement process lengthy and costly, particularly if probate court becomes involved. To help ease the burden, here are five essential tips for siblings managing inherited property in Wisconsin.

One Person in Charge

When a will lacks specific instructions or a designated trustee, one of the first steps is to decide who will take the lead. Having a single sibling act as the coordinator can help streamline communication and ensure that everyone stays on track. This person—ideally someone organized and impartial—can maintain a central checklist and oversee progress on tasks like paying property taxes, managing upkeep, and handling legal matters.

To avoid overburdening the coordinator, siblings can divide responsibilities. For instance, one sibling might manage routine maintenance while another handles financial aspects. If the property proves too challenging to manage, hiring a property manager and splitting the expenses may be a practical solution. A well-organized approach prevents tasks from falling through the cracks and minimizes potential disagreements.

Stay Civil

It’s a sad reality that nearly 70% of Americans pass away without a will, leaving heirs to navigate estate issues on their own. For siblings dealing with an inherited property in Wisconsin, open communication is essential. Transparent discussions help set clear expectations and avoid misunderstandings. However, emotions can run high, and it’s not uncommon for disagreements to arise. If conversations become heated or unproductive, consider involving a neutral third party, such as a mediator, to guide the discussion.

The earlier these conversations take place, the better. Establishing guidelines and addressing sensitive topics upfront can help, preserve familial relationships. Remember, the ultimate goal is not only to resolve property issues but also to maintain harmony within the family.

Right Motivations

The distribution of an estate can sometimes create tension among siblings, particularly if the division feels unequal or unfair. Parents may have had various reasons for their decisions, such as addressing the unique financial needs of one child or expressing concerns about a child’s spouse. In other cases, parents may opt for an equal split, hoping to avoid conflict. Unfortunately, misunderstandings or perceived favoritism can lead to emotional disputes.

Taking the time to understand the motivations behind your parents’ decisions can be incredibly helpful. When siblings approach the situation with empathy and a willingness to listen, they’re more likely to find common ground. Open discussions about the reasoning behind the estate plan can help alleviate feelings of resentment and foster cooperation.

Establish Fair Rules

Disagreements are inevitable when multiple people share ownership of a property. Being realistic about potential conflicts allows siblings to establish a framework for decision-making that everyone views as fair. For example, creating a majority-rule system can simplify decisions on matters ranging from minor repairs to significant issues like whether one sibling can live in the property full-time.

By setting these rules early, you can prevent emotional disputes from derailing progress. Focus on crafting guidelines that prioritize practicality and fairness. Remember, while emotions may run high in the moment, decisions should ultimately serve the long-term interests of everyone involved.

Sell and Divide 

In some cases, selling the property may be the most practical solution. If the financial or emotional burden of co-ownership becomes too great, selling allows siblings to divide the proceeds and move forward. This option can be particularly appealing if:

  • The property requires extensive repairs or upkeep.
  • One or more siblings cannot afford their share of the financial responsibilities.
  • None of the siblings have a personal use for the property.
  • The property is nearing foreclosure and needs to be sold quickly.

Selling can also prevent long-term conflicts and provide each sibling with financial flexibility.
