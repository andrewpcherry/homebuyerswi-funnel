Racine, Wisconsin has one of the oldest housing stocks in the region — the city’s own 2025 housing study puts the median dwelling age at nearly 60 years, with only about 13.4% of homes built since 1980. That age profile drives most of the issues buyers and owners run into.

Common issues in Racine’s housing stock:

  • Lead water service lines — this is a live, documented issue: the city is in the middle of a $130 million project to replace roughly 10,000 lead service lines (the underground pipes connecting individual homes to the water main), work that began in May 2026 and is slated to run over five years. Lead lines were standard practice for decades and are common in a city this old.
  • Lead-based paint — with the bulk of the housing stock predating 1980, and a historic core dating to the 1840s-1920s, lead paint in older layers of interior and exterior paint is a routine hazard in homes that haven’t been remediated.
  • Outdated electrical systems — homes built before 1950 (common in Racine’s older neighborhoods) frequently still have knob-and-tube wiring, where the original rubber insulation has become brittle and flakes off, exposing bare wire; homes from the 1960s-70s often have aluminum wiring instead, which loosens at outlets and switches from thermal expansion and lacks a dedicated ground. Many older homes also still run on undersized 60- or 100-amp electrical panels inadequate for modern appliance loads.
  • Aging heating systems — older Midwestern housing stock of this era commonly used boilers and radiators rather than forced-air furnaces; both boiler and furnace equipment installed decades ago is now well past typical service life in a lot of Racine homes.
  • Roofing at or past end of life — with a median home age near 60 years, a large share of roofs are old enough to need replacement or have already been replaced at least once, making roof condition a frequent inspection issue.
  • Foundation and basement moisture problems — older masonry foundations combined with Wisconsin’s freeze-thaw cycles commonly produce cracking, settling, and water infiltration, especially in basements of homes built before modern waterproofing standards.
  • Deteriorating masonry and tuckpointing needs — Racine has a substantial stock of older brick homes, particularly in historic areas like the Southside Historic District (homes dating 1842-1924), where aging mortar joints commonly need repointing to prevent water damage.
  • Inefficient windows and insulation — pre-1980 construction typically means original single-pane windows and minimal wall/attic insulation, contributing to drafts and, in winter, ice damming on older roofs.
  • Possible asbestos-containing materials — insulation, floor tile, and siding materials common before the 1980s can contain asbestos, a consideration during renovation or demolition of older homes.
